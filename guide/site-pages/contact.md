# The Contact Page

The whole point of a public website is that people can reach you.  The contact page makes that easy for humans — without making it easy for robots.

## Use the form

Your site includes a contact form: a visitor types a message, it arrives in email, and your address is never displayed.  This matters because an email address published as plain text on a public page gets found by automated spam harvesters within days, and it stays on their lists forever.  The form keeps the channel open and the address hidden.

Two habits keep the form trustworthy:

- **Test it now and then** — send yourself a message and confirm it arrives.  A silently broken contact form is a collaborator lost without a trace.
- **Know who receives it**, and update that when people leave the group.  A form delivering to a graduated student's abandoned inbox is the same as no form at all.

## Where to find you

- **Building, room, and campus** — with a link to the university's map.
- **Mailing address and shipping address, both.**  Labs receive packages, and at most universities the address for a letter and the address for a crate of equipment are not the same.  Listing both saves your vendors a phone call and saves you a lost delivery.
- **A phone number, only if someone actually answers it.**

## Social media

Link only the accounts that are alive.  A feed that last posted three years ago does more harm than no link at all — it is the digital equivalent of a dusty storefront.  One living account beats four abandoned ones.
