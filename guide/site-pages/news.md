# The News Page

The news page is optional, and that is the most important thing to know about it.  It is the one page on your site with dates on everything, and dates cut both ways.

## How it works

You do not edit the news page directly.  You write [posts](../how-it-works/pages-and-posts.md), and they collect here automatically, newest first.  Over time the page becomes the running history of your group — which is lovely, as long as the history keeps running.

## What counts as news

More than you think.  A paper accepted.  An award or fellowship.  A dissertation defended — with the traditional triumphant photo.  New members joining.  A conference the group presented at.  An outreach event.  Press coverage.

A news post is not an essay.  Three sentences and a photo is a complete post: what happened, who did it, why it is worth a smile.  Done.

One writing tip: name your students.  "Congratulations to Priya Shah on her successful defense" is a news post, a morale boost, and the search result her family finds — all in one sentence.

## The staleness problem

Here is the honest question to ask before creating this page: will someone in your group post at least a few times a year?

If yes, wonderful — a steady trickle of dated posts is the strongest possible signal that your lab is alive.  If no, skip the news page entirely.  A site without a news page looks deliberate and complete.  A news page whose most recent item is three years old shouts "abandoned" louder than anything else on your site — the dates make sure of it.

There is no shame in either answer.  There is only shame in the three-year-old post.

## The good news about news

If you do keep the page, the academic calendar writes it for you.  Students arrive in fall.  Papers land all year.  Defenses cluster in spring.  You never have to invent news — you only have to write down the good things within a week of them happening, while the photo is still on someone's phone.
