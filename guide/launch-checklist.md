# The Launch Checklist

Before your site goes public, walk through this list.  Better yet, recruit someone who has never seen the site — a colleague from another department, a new student — and watch them use it.  What confuses them will confuse everyone.

- [ ] Every page has real content.  No placeholder text left anywhere — search the site for "lorem ipsum" and for your own bracketed notes-to-self.
- [ ] Names and titles are spelled correctly.  People forgive most things; they do not forgive their own name misspelled.
- [ ] Every image has alt text (see [accessibility](how-it-works/accessibility.md)).
- [ ] The contact form works — submit a test message and confirm it arrives in the right inbox.
- [ ] Every link has been clicked, including the ones to papers and profiles.
- [ ] The whole site has been read on a phone.  A large share of your visitors will only ever see it there.
- [ ] The homepage's one-sentence description passes the neighbor test (see [writing for visitors](how-it-works/writing-for-the-web.md)).
- [ ] Someone outside the group has read it and told you what was confusing.

When everything checks out, tell us and we will make the site public and visible to search engines.

## After launch

Twice a year — start of fall, start of spring — spend fifteen minutes: update the [people page](site-pages/people.md), import new [publications](site-pages/publications.md), check that the [join us page](site-pages/join-us.md) still tells the truth about openings.  That small ritual is the entire difference between a site that recruits for you and a site that quietly embarrasses you.

That is the whole secret.  Launch is a morning; the habit is fifteen minutes a semester.
