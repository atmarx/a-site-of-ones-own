# Writing for Visitors

Nobody reads websites.  They skim them — in about ten seconds a visitor decides whether your page has what they came for, and only then do they slow down.  Writing for the web means writing for that skim.

## Front-load everything

Put the point in the first sentence — of the page, and of every paragraph.  Academic writing builds to a conclusion; web writing leads with it.  A visitor who only reads your first sentence should still leave knowing what your group does.

## Write for the smart outsider

The reader of your site is almost never a specialist in your subfield.  It is a prospective student who has not taken your course yet, a program officer from a neighboring discipline, a journalist on deadline, or a proud parent.  Describe your research the way you would explain it to a sharp colleague from a different department: accurately, but without assuming your vocabulary.

A useful test: could a neighbor read your homepage and tell someone else what your lab does?  If not, simplify until they could.  You are not dumbing anything down — the precise version lives in your publications, one click away.

## Spell out acronyms

Write the full term the first time it appears, with the acronym after it in parentheses, and only then use the acronym.  You know exactly what your field's abbreviations mean; a first-year student does not, and neither does the reviewer from the adjacent panel.  You will notice this guide plays by the same rule.

## Keep it short and structured

Short paragraphs — two to four sentences.  Headings as signposts, so skimmers can find their section.  Lists when you are listing.  If a page needs scrolling and scrolling, it probably wants to be two pages.

## Freshness is credibility

A website last touched in 2021 whispers that the lab might have been, too.  You do not need to write constantly — you need the [people](../site-pages/people.md), [research](../site-pages/research.md), and [publications](../site-pages/publications.md) pages to be true.  Put a fifteen-minute review on your calendar at the start of each semester, and your site will feel alive with almost no effort at all.
