# The Join Us Page

This is the most-read page you were probably not planning to make.  Prospective graduate students and postdoctoral researchers read the join us page harder than anything else on your site — it is where they decide whether to write to you.  It is also where you save yourself from a hundred identical emails.

## Say whether you are recruiting

At the top of the page, plainly: "We are currently recruiting graduate students for fall 2027" or "We have no funded openings at the moment, but we welcome inquiries from students applying for fellowships."  Then keep it true — update this line every semester.  A stale "we're hiring!" is worse than an honest "not right now," because the student who writes an eager email into silence tells their friends.

## For prospective graduate students

Explain the mechanics, because they are genuinely confusing from the outside: in most programs, students apply to the university's graduate program, not to an individual lab — link to the program's official admissions page and say so.  Then say what you look for in a group member: coursework, skills, curiosity about particular problems, whatever is actually true for your group.

## Ask for a real email

Tell prospective members exactly what to send you: a CV, a note on their background, and — this is the important one — a few sentences on which of your projects or papers interests them and why.

This one instruction transforms your inbox.  Mass-mailed "Dear Professor, I am deeply interested in your esteemed research" messages ignore the instruction and identify themselves on sight.  Genuine applicants follow it, and their emails arrive pre-sorted and easy to answer.  Everyone wins, including the genuine applicants.

## Undergraduates and postdoctoral researchers

For undergraduates: say whether you take them, what the arrangement usually is (course credit, paid positions, volunteer hours), and when in the year to ask.

For postdocs: be honest about funding.  If you have a funded opening, post it here.  If not, say whether you welcome candidates who want to apply for fellowships together — for a strong candidate, an offer to support a fellowship application is a real invitation.

## When you are not hiring

Keep the page anyway.  Say how to be considered when something opens.  The student you will want in two years is reading this page tonight — leave the light on for them.
