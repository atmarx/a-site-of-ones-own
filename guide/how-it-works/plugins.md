# Plugins

A plugin is an add-on that teaches WordPress a new trick, the way an app adds an ability to your phone.  Some people call them extensions — same idea.  Your people directory, your publications list, your contact form: each is powered by a plugin.

## We install and maintain them for you

Plugins are software, and software needs updating.  We install plugins from a supported catalog, and we update every site centrally, so nothing quietly rots.

This is also why you cannot install plugins yourself: every plugin is code running on a shared server, and a plugin that is safe today can become a security problem next year if its developer walks away.  The catalog is our promise that everything on your site will keep working — and our promise to know how to fix any of it when you call.

## What is in the catalog

The plugins you will actually notice:

- **Team Members** — powers your [people page](../site-pages/people.md).  Each lab member is an entry with a photo, a role, and links; the plugin arranges them into a tidy grid.
- **teachPress** — powers your [publications page](../site-pages/publications.md).  It imports your reference list and formats it by year, and it can handle hundreds of entries without complaint.
- **A contact form** — lets visitors send you a message from your [contact page](../site-pages/contact.md) without your email address ever being published where spammers can harvest it.
- **An accessibility checker** — reads over your shoulder while you edit and flags problems in plain English.  More on this in [accessibility](accessibility.md).
- **An events calendar** — available on request, if your group runs a seminar series or hosts events worth listing.

## What you do not need — because it is already handled

If you have run a WordPress site before, you may expect to need plugins for backups, security scanning, or performance.  You do not.  Backups happen automatically at the hosting level.  Security is handled by us, below the level any plugin can reach — adding a per-site security plugin would actually slow your site down without making it safer.  If a well-meaning colleague tells you every WordPress site needs one of these, you can tell them yours came with all of it built in.

## Requesting something new

If your group needs something the catalog does not cover, ask.  When we evaluate a plugin we look at three things: is it actively maintained, does it have a safe track record, and does something already in the catalog do the same job.  Sometimes the answer is yes and it joins the catalog.  Sometimes the honest answer is that the plugin will become a problem in two years — and in that case we will say so and help you find another route to what you actually need.
