# Users: Who Can Log In

Here is the most common point of confusion about lab websites, so let us clear it up first: the people listed *on* your website are not people who log *into* your website.

Your students and staff appear on your [people page](../site-pages/people.md) the way they might appear in a photo on the wall — being displayed there does not require an account.  One person with an account maintains the whole roster.  Most research groups need only two or three accounts, total.

## The two roles that matter

Every account has a role that sets what it can do:

- **Editor** — can create and change any page or post on the site.  This is the right role for anyone who maintains content, and it is the default we assign.
- **Administrator** — everything an Editor can do, plus site settings and user management.  Usually this is our support team plus, if desired, the principal investigator.

There are other roles with narrower powers, but for a research group site they rarely earn their keep.

## Our recommendation

Give Editor accounts to the principal investigator and one or two trusted delegates — a lab manager, a coordinator, or a senior student who has agreed to keep the site current.  That is it.

Every account is a door into the site.  The fewer doors, the fewer keys to track, and the less to clean up when people move on.  A site with two careful editors stays in better shape than a site where everyone can log in and nobody is responsible.

## When someone leaves

When a person with an account leaves the group, tell us and we will remove the account.  Their entry on the people page can stay — in fact, moving them to the alumni section is exactly the right thing to do, and the [people page](../site-pages/people.md) explains why alumni are worth showing off.

Accounts and content are separate on purpose.  Students graduate, staff move on, and the site sails forward regardless.
