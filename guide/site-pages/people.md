# The People Page

After the homepage, this is the most visited page on any lab site.  Prospective students want to see who they would be working beside.  Colleagues look for familiar faces.  Your own group members send the link to their families.  Fortunately, it is also the easiest page to keep current.

## How it works

Your people page is powered by the Team Members plugin.  Each person is an entry — photo, name, role, a line or two of description, links — and the plugin arranges the entries into a tidy grid.  Adding a new member means adding one entry; the layout takes care of itself.  You never rebuild the page.

## Group people by role

The usual sections, in the usual order:

- Principal Investigator — the faculty member who leads the group
- Postdoctoral researchers
- Graduate students
- Undergraduate students
- Staff
- Alumni

Use the sections your group actually has, and skip the rest.

## For each person

- **A real photo.**  See the consent note in [images and photos](../how-it-works/images-and-media.md) — ask before publishing, especially for students.
- **Name and role.**
- **A sentence or two** about what they work on.
- **Links, as appropriate**: a Google Scholar profile, an ORCID (a free identifier researchers use so their work is never confused with a similarly named colleague's), a GitHub profile for people who publish code, or a personal website.
- **Email is optional.**  Listing every member's address invites spam their way; many groups list only the principal investigator's contact and let the [contact page](contact.md) handle the rest.  Ask your members what they prefer.

## The photo trick

Photograph everyone against the same wall, on the same day — ten minutes at the end of a group meeting does it.  A grid of consistent photos looks instantly professional.  A mix of conference headshots, cropped party photos, and gray "no photo" silhouettes reads as neglect, no matter how good the science is.

## Alumni: the section that recruits for you

When someone leaves, do not delete them — move them to alumni, with a line about where they went: "Now a data scientist at...", "Now an assistant professor at...".  Prospective students read the alumni list closely, and what it tells them is *people who train here go on to good things*.  It is the cheapest recruiting you will ever do, and it costs one line per person per career.
