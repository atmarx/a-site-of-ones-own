# The Principal Investigator's Page

"Principal Investigator" — usually shortened to PI — is the academic term for the faculty member who leads a research group.  This page is the PI's professional profile: a readable, public version of the academic résumé.  It is the page seminar hosts check before writing an introduction, journalists check before a call, and committees check before a meeting.

## Write it in third person

"Dr. Rivera is a professor of..." rather than "I am..."  The reason is practical: people copy this page.  A seminar host pastes it into their introduction; a conference program lifts it verbatim; a journalist quotes it.  Written in third person, it is ready to reuse — which means you get to control exactly how you are introduced.  Think of this page as the introduction you would write for yourself, and write a good one.

## What to include

- **A professional photo.**
- **Current title and appointments.**
- **A short biography** — one to three paragraphs: the arc of the research in plain words, the career path in brief, and the honors a stranger would recognize.  Selective beats complete.
- **Education** — degrees, institutions, years.
- **A few honors and awards.**  The ones that mean something outside your subfield.  The exhaustive list belongs in the full CV.
- **A handful of selected publications** — five or so favorites with links, for the reader who wants a taste.  The complete record lives on the [publications page](publications.md).
- **A link to the full CV as a downloadable file.**  CV is short for curriculum vitae — the complete academic record of degrees, positions, papers, talks, and service.  The full thirty-page version should be a PDF file to download, not a webpage; put "Updated [month, year]" at the top of it so readers know it is current, and remember to keep that true.
- **Contact information**, or a link to the [contact page](contact.md).

## What to leave off

Anything the homepage already says — this page complements the site, it does not repeat it.  And resist the urge to reproduce the entire CV as a webpage: a visitor who wants all thirty pages will happily download them, and everyone else gets a page they can actually read.
