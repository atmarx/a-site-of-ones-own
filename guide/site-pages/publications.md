# The Publications Page

For colleagues, reviewers, and serious prospective students, the publications page is the proof of life: it shows the group produces.  Your site keeps the list itself, using the teachPress plugin, and once the first import is done, maintaining it takes minutes a semester.

## Where the list comes from

You almost certainly already have your publication list in a citation manager — Zotero, EndNote, Mendeley, or a Google Scholar profile.  Every one of them can export a **BibTeX file**: a plain text format for reference lists that the academic world has used for decades.

The workflow is: export the BibTeX file from wherever your references live, import it into teachPress, done.  The plugin formats everything into a list grouped by year, and it handles hundreds of entries without complaint.  The first import is the big one; after that, you only add what is new.

## Keeping it current

Two approaches, both fine:

- Add each paper as it is accepted, using the plugin's entry form — a two-minute job while the good news is fresh.
- Or do a bulk import once a semester from a fresh BibTeX export.

Pick whichever ritual you will actually keep, and put it on the calendar.  The publications page is the first place a savvy visitor checks for signs of life.

## Link to the papers

Every published paper has a **DOI** — a Digital Object Identifier, the permanent link that publishers assign so a paper can always be found even if a journal's website reorganizes.  Include it for each entry; teachPress displays it as a link.

Where your publishing agreement allows, also link a freely readable copy — many universities run a repository for exactly this, and your library can tell you what you are allowed to post.  A visitor who cannot get past a paywall is a reader lost for no reason.

## Why the list lives on your site

It is tempting to have the page pull automatically from a Google Scholar profile or someone's citation-manager account.  We recommend against it, and here is the lifecycle problem in one sentence: automatic feeds are tied to a person's account, and they break when that person graduates, leaves, or loses interest.

Your publication list should outlive every current member of your group — including, eventually, the software fashions of 2026.  The way to guarantee that is to keep the list on your own site, in a format as old and boring and reliable as BibTeX.  Boring is a feature.
