# The Equipment Page

Not every group needs this page — and if yours has no instruments or facilities worth showing, skip it without guilt.  But if your lab has capabilities other people would care about, an equipment page quietly does a lot of work.

Three kinds of readers come here: a potential collaborator wondering whether you can measure the thing they need measured, a prospective student sizing up where the good instruments are (this is a real factor, and there is no shame in it), and a program officer confirming your group can actually do what your proposal says.

## For each instrument or facility

- **A real photo** — of your machine in your lab, not the manufacturer's catalog picture.  Your slightly cluttered bench is more convincing than their showroom.
- **Name and model.**
- **What it does, in plain words.**  "Measures the composition of a sample's surface, layer by layer" first; the terminology can follow.
- **A capability or two that matters** — resolution, sample types, size limits, throughput.  This page's readers can handle a specification; just lead with the plain-words version.
- **Availability.**  Can outside groups use it?  Through collaboration, or for a fee?  Whom do they contact?  If the answer is "it depends," say who to ask — an instrument that invites inquiries earns collaborations.

## A bonus you will thank yourself for

Grant applications routinely require a description of your facilities and resources.  A well-kept equipment page is a standing first draft of that section — write it once here, and every future proposal starts warm.

## Keep it honest

When an instrument is decommissioned, sold, or permanently broken, take it off the page.  A collaborator who writes to ask about a machine that left the building in 2019 will not write twice.
