# Pages and Posts

WordPress gives you two kinds of content, and knowing which one to reach for is half the battle.

**Pages** are the permanent parts of your site: the homepage, the research overview, the people directory, the contact page.  They appear in your site's menu, they do not carry a date, and visitors expect them to be current whenever they arrive.

**Posts** are dated announcements: a paper accepted, an award won, a student's dissertation defended.  Each post is stamped with the day you wrote it, and posts collect automatically on your [news page](../site-pages/news.md), newest first.

## The rule of thumb

If it will still be true a year from now, it is a page.  If it is an announcement, it is a post.

| You want to add... | Use a... |
|---|---|
| A description of a research project | Page — it belongs on your [research page](../site-pages/research.md) |
| "Our paper was accepted at..." | Post |
| A new lab member | An entry on your [people page](../site-pages/people.md) |
| "Maria defended her dissertation!" | Post |
| Directions to the lab | Page — the [contact page](../site-pages/contact.md) |

## Where they show up

Pages live in your site's menu, the navigation visitors see at the top of every page.  When you add a page, tell us (or your site's administrator) if it should appear in the menu — not every page needs to.

Posts take care of themselves.  You never add a post to a menu; it simply appears on the news page and, eventually, scrolls down into history as newer posts arrive.  That is exactly what news is supposed to do.
